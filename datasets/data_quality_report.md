### A3. Cleaning decisions already made by the data owner (Read_Me sheet)
- **Scope**: 37 respondents explicitly consented; non-consenting responses are excluded.
- **Modelling cohort**: 33 respondents retained; 4 respondents with reported cycle length under 18 days excluded from the cycle-length modelling cohort as likely bleeding-duration misinterpretation.
- **Important limitation**: The <18-day rule is a data-cleaning assumption based on the question context and very short values. It is not a medical diagnosis. The excluded responses remain available in the audit sheet.
- **Date alignment**: Each participant's own entered period dates are sorted newest-to-oldest by full date.
- **Same day each month**: Dates such as 5 Jan, 5 Feb and 5 Mar are different full dates and are all retained. A recurring day-of-month is not a duplicate.
- **Exact repeated date**: If the exact same full date (same day, month and year) appears in multiple date fields, it is counted once only when calculating consecutive intervals. The original date entries are retained in the audit columns.
- **Cycle interval**: Date-based interval = newer period start date minus the next older distinct period start date.
- **No fabricated corrections**: No missing or suspicious date has been changed or invented. Unusual date intervals remain flagged for review.
- **Ranges**: Self-reported ranges (e.g. 27–29) are represented by their midpoint in the provisional numeric column; the raw answer is preserved.
- **Privacy**: Names and submission timestamps are removed; anonymous participant IDs are used.
- **Model readiness**: This is a cleaned primary-data working cohort, not a fully validated final model dataset. Date and target consistency still need review.

### A6. Can the primary dataset train a prediction model?
- Only 24 people have 2+ intervals and 6 have 3. Total 63 intervals from 33 people, spanning one short window.
- That is far too little for a supervised next-cycle model, and after the out-of-range
  intervals are set aside it is even less. **Recommended role: describe the student
  population, test the app's rules on realistic student data, and (at most) act as a
  small external sanity-check set. NOT a training set.**
- It has NO BMI, sleep, stress or exam-period fields, so the lifestyle module can only
  use symptoms, diet, exercise and activity from here; richer lifestyle analysis must
  come from app users over time.

## PART C - Are the two datasets compatible for combined training?

| Aspect | Primary survey | Kaggle / FedCycle |
|---|---|---|
| Unit of observation | 1 row = 1 person | 1 row = 1 cycle of 1 person |
| Participants | 33 (cohort) | 159 |
| Cycle information | Up to 4 period START dates -> max 3 intervals | Cycle length for many consecutive cycles (no dates) |
| Age | 18-45, median 22 | 21-43, median 31 |
| Bleeding duration | Category (2-3, 4-5, 6-7, 8+ days) | Exact days (LengthofMenses) |
| Symptoms / diet / exercise / activity | Yes (self-reported, categorical) | No (only bleeding scores) |
| BMI | No | Only 128 participants |
| How cycles were recorded | Recalled dates typed in a survey | Appears to be prospective charting for fertility awareness (inferred from column names; confirm with codebook) |
| Time window | About 4 months (2026-06-10 to 2026-10-22) | Many cycles per person over a long period |

**Genuinely common variables:** age, cycle length, bleeding (length) duration. Nothing else is shared.

**Verdict (my reading of the numbers above):**
1. Do NOT merge them row-by-row. Different unit of observation, different recording method,
   and the primary survey cannot supply the multi-cycle history a sequence model needs.
2. Kaggle/FedCycle is the only realistic TRAINING source for cycle-interval prediction, using
   only features that exist in the app too (previous cycle lengths and simple summaries,
   age if available). Participant-level split by ClientID.
3. The primary survey is used for (a) describing the student population, (b) testing the
   irregularity rules, (c) a tiny, clearly-labelled sanity check of the trained model.
4. Population caveat: Kaggle participants are older on average and appear to have been charting for
   fertility awareness; the primary survey is a small student sample. So the model must be
   described as 'trained on an adult fertility-charting dataset', NOT 'trained on Indian
   college students'.
