Menstrual health, explained

Short, plain-language guides. General education based on public guidance from ACOG, FIGO, NHS and WHO - not a substitute for a doctor.

The four phases

Days 1-5 or so

Menstrual

Bleeding as the womb lining sheds. Day 1 is the first day of bleeding. Cramps and tiredness are common.

After bleeding

Follicular

The body prepares an egg. Energy and mood often lift.

Around the middle

Mid-cycle (ovulation)

An egg is released, often about 14 days before the next period. Timing varies a lot between people.

Before the next period

Luteal

Hormones rise then fall. PMS symptoms like bloating, cravings and mood changes can appear.

Phases shown in Let It Flow are estimated from day count only and are for education.

What does cycle length mean?

Cycle length is counted from the first day of one period to the day before the first day of the next. Periods starting on 1 March and 29 March mean a 28-day cycle.

For most adults, 24-38 days (often quoted as 21-35) is the usual range. In the first few years after periods begin, up to 45 days can be normal. A few days of month-to-month difference is normal; the gap between your shortest and longest recent cycle is commonly up to about 7-9 days.

Common period symptoms
  • Cramps from womb muscle contractions - usually strongest in the first 1-2 days.
  • Bloating, breast tenderness, cravings before and during a period.
  • Tiredness from disturbed sleep and some iron loss.
  • Headaches as hormone levels shift.
  • Mood changes and acne in the days before bleeding.
Why cycles vary

Cycles can change with stress (including exams), poor or irregular sleep, illness, travel or hostel routine changes, quick weight change, very intense exercise, skipping meals or strict dieting, and some medicines. Early in the reproductive years, cycles are also naturally less regular.

These factors are associated with variation - they don't mean every irregular cycle has an obvious cause. Medical conditions can also affect cycles, which is why persistent changes deserve a doctor's opinion.

When to talk to a healthcare professional
  • Your cycles are often shorter than 21 days or longer than 35-45 days.
  • You miss several periods, or go 3 months without one (and aren't pregnant).
  • Your cycle length varies by more than about 9 days again and again.
  • Your periods last more than 7 days.
  • You bleed between periods or after sex.
  • Periods regularly stop you attending class or daily activities.
Severe pain, heavy bleeding: don't wait
Seek medical care promptly if: you soak through a pad or tampon every hour for 2+ hours; pass clots bigger than a coin; feel dizzy, faint or breathless; have pain that is severe, worsening or not eased by usual measures; or have fever with pelvic pain. In an emergency call 112.
Period hygiene and everyday care
  • Change pads every 4-6 hours, tampons at least every 4-8 hours.
  • Wash hands, use plain water for the outer genital area and avoid scented products.
  • Warmth, light movement, water and rest help many people with cramps.
  • Keep a small kit in your bag during exams and travel.
